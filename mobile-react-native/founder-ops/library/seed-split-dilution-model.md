# Seed split: ₹5 cr angel iSAFE plus ₹21 cr institutional round

The question: what valuation do we pitch the angels, so that the angels and the Q1 2027 institutional cheque together dilute us by no more than 23%, and ideally 18 to 20%.

The live calculator is at https://claude.ai/artifact/Ciyr53bMWYQwH1Hj1CMVSw (type the institution's number, switch scenarios). It runs the same model.

Every table below is generated by `founder-ops/calc/split.py` and checked against a share-by-share cap table. Rerun `python3 calc/build_split_doc.py` from the founder-ops folder to refresh it after changing an input.

## The answer

**Pitch the angels a ₹85 crore post-money cap with a 10% discount.** On the iSAFE the angels convert at whichever is lower, the cap price or the discount price, inside the institutional round.

- It is the lowest round-number cap that keeps total seed dilution under 23% when the institutional round prices at our bear case (₹116 cr post-money). The exact floor is ₹83.6 cr.
- At our base case (₹130 cr post-money) the total is about 21%. At the bull case (₹153 cr) it is under 19%.
- Angels still get a real step-up: roughly 1.1x if the round is weak, 1.3x at base and 1.5x at bull, on top of being first in.
- My earlier suggestion of a ₹75 cr cap was made before 23% became a hard ceiling. It only holds the ceiling if the institutional post-money is at least ₹120 cr. At ₹116 cr it gives 23.6%.

| Case | Institutional post-money | VC pre-money | VC gets | Angels get | Total seed dilution | If angels paid the round price | Cost of the angel discount | Founders after seed | Pool after seed | Angel step-up |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Bear | ₹116 cr | ₹95 cr | 18.1% | 4.8% | **22.9%** | 22.4% | 0.5 pts | 69.4% | 7.7% | 1.12x |
| Base | ₹130 cr | ₹109 cr | 16.2% | 4.9% | **21.1%** | 20.0% | 1.1 pts | 71.0% | 7.9% | 1.28x |
| Bull | ₹153 cr | ₹132 cr | 13.7% | 5.1% | **18.8%** | 17.0% | 1.8 pts | 73.1% | 8.1% | 1.55x |

Post-money here means the value of the company the moment the whole seed has closed, angels included. VC pre-money is that number less the ₹21 cr cheque, and the angels' converted shares sit inside it.

## What the 23% ceiling really depends on

Most of the dilution is set by the institutional valuation, not by the angel cap. Raising ₹26 cr at a post-money of V dilutes us by 26 / V even if the angels paid exactly the round price. The angel cap only adds a small premium on top of that.

| Total dilution target | Post-money needed (angels at round price) | VC pre-money for the ₹21 cr |
|---|---|---|
| 23% | ₹113 cr | ₹92 cr |
| 20% | ₹130 cr | ₹109 cr |
| 18% | ₹144 cr | ₹123 cr |

So there are two honest statements to make to ourselves:

1. **The 23% ceiling needs an institutional post-money of about ₹113 cr or more, whatever cap we give the angels.** Below that the only fix is a smaller institutional cheque (see the cheque table further down).
2. **The 18 to 20% band is a valuation outcome, not a cap outcome.** Twenty percent needs ₹130 cr post-money with no angel discount at all. With the ₹85 cr cap it needs about ₹140 cr post-money (VC pre-money near ₹119 cr). The cap decides how much of the gap we give away to angels, usually 1 to 3 points.

## Inputs and assumptions

- Round: ₹26 cr in two parts, ₹5 cr angels on iSAFE now, ₹21 cr institutional in Q1 2027. (At about $2.7M the implied rate is roughly ₹96 a dollar, so quote angels in rupees and check the rate before putting dollars in writing.)
- Before the seed the founders hold 90% and the option pool 10%, the same basis as the financial model and `our-story.md`. No extra pool top-up at the seed; the model adds 5% at the Series A.
- The iSAFE is drafted on a post-money cap (TrailBlazer's read of the 100X.VC template). If your lawyer's template uses a pre-money cap, subtract ₹5 cr from every cap in this document to get the equivalent.
- The iSAFE converts inside the institutional pre-money, which is how institutions price SAFEs. Make the term sheet say so in writing, and quote the round to us as a post-money.
- Scenario valuations are the financial model's seed cases: bear ₹116 cr, base ₹130 cr, bull ₹153 cr post-money. They are our planning numbers, not a market read. Nobody can promise a Q1 2027 valuation.

## How the numbers work

```
I = angel cheque (5)   X = institutional cheque (21)   C = angel cap   d = discount
V = post-money after the whole seed   P = V - X = institutional pre-money

angel share if the cap binds       = (I / C) x (P / V)
angel share if the discount binds  = (I / (1 - d)) / V
angel share at the round price     = I / V          (the floor)
angel share                        = the largest of the three
institutional share                = X / V
total seed dilution                = angel share + institutional share
founders after seed                = 90% x (1 - total dilution)
angel step-up                      = angel share x V / I
lowest cap that holds a ceiling D  = I x (V - X) / (D x V - X)
```

The cap binds when the institutional pre-money is more than about 11% above the cap (that is, when the cap price is below the discount price). Below that the discount takes over and gives angels a small floor.

## Lowest cap that holds the ceiling

For each institutional post-money, the lowest cap we can give the angels and still land under the target (with a 10% discount in the note). "None" means no cap can get there, because the round price plus the 10% discount floor is already over target. Dropping the discount lowers that floor.

| Institutional post-money | Hold 23% | Hold 22% | Hold 21% | Hold 20% |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| ₹113 cr | None | None | None | None |
| ₹116 cr | ₹84 cr | None | None | None |
| ₹120 cr | ₹75 cr | None | None | None |
| ₹125 cr | ₹67 cr | ₹80 cr | None | None |
| ₹130 cr | ₹61 cr | ₹72 cr | ₹87 cr | None |
| ₹140 cr | ₹53 cr | ₹61 cr | ₹71 cr | ₹85 cr |
| ₹150 cr | ₹48 cr | ₹54 cr | ₹61 cr | ₹72 cr |

Read it as: if we expect the institutional round to land at ₹120 cr or better, ₹75 cr is enough to hold 23%. We recommend ₹85 cr because it still holds when the round lands as low as ₹116 cr, which is the bear case.

One tactic to consider: a tiered close. The first ₹2 cr of angels at a ₹80 cr cap, the remaining ₹3 cr at ₹90 cr. It rewards the people who commit first, and the blended result is almost identical to a single ₹85 cr cap (5.83% against 5.88% before the institutional round dilutes it). The iSAFE documents are separate per investor, so it needs no special drafting, but tell every angel the structure up front.

## What to tell the angels

- **The line:** "You come in at a ₹85 crore cap. We are aiming to close the institutional seed in Q1 at a materially higher valuation, so you convert at a discount to that round, and you are in before the January launch." Never quote them a target valuation for the institutional round as a promise.
- **How far below the round the cap sits:** ₹85 cr is about 27% below ₹116 cr, 35% below ₹130 cr and 44% below ₹153 cr.
- **Why not lower:** every point of cap we give away comes out of the founders. A cap far below the round price also invites the institution to ask us to reprice the angels, which costs us goodwill we need.
- **Why a discount as well:** it protects the angels if the Q1 round is flat or weak. At ₹85 cr it only matters when the institutional post-money is below about ₹115 cr.
- **What they actually hold in the meantime:** CCPS under the iSAFE, with a liquidation preference up to their invested capital, converting at the next priced round or after three years, whichever comes first.

## Checks before the documents go out

1. **Cap definition.** Ask the lawyer to confirm the template's cap is post-money and quote the clause. This whole document shifts by ₹5 cr if it is pre-money.
2. **Conversion trigger.** The institutional round must clearly count as the equity financing that converts the iSAFE. Do not let a minimum round size in the note exclude a smaller institutional close.
3. **Who can hold an iSAFE.** One law-firm source says it is for Indian investors only, another covers foreign investors under FEMA. If any angel is an NRI or overseas, ask counsel which instrument and pricing rules apply.
4. **Pool top-up.** An institution may ask for a bigger pool in the pre-money. That comes out of the founders and sits outside every number here, so negotiate it as its own line.
5. **MFN and side letters.** If any angel gets a better term, others may be able to claim it. Keep terms uniform, or use the tiered structure openly.
6. **Angel tax.** Section 56(2)(viib) was abolished from assessment year 2025-26, so pricing above fair value is no longer an angel-tax problem. Confirm with the CA before closing.
7. **Keep the cap out of the collateral.** The decks, the two-pager and the outreach emails quote ₹26 cr and the ₹5 cr and ₹21 cr split with no valuation. Leave it that way, and give the ₹85 cr figure only in conversation and in the iSAFE itself.

This is a planning model, not legal or tax advice. The figures move the moment an institution names a number, so rerun the script with the real quote.
