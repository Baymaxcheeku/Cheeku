> Notes: Indian prescriptions are handwritten, brand-named and short on explanation. Older parents collect medicines from three or four doctors who never see each other's lists. Cheeku reads every prescription down to its salts and runs ten checks against published standards: banned combinations, interactions, dose for the patient's kidney function, duplicates, antibiotic use, elderly safety, monitoring tests and cost. The family gets a scorecard and the questions to ask their doctor. It never tells anyone to stop a medicine, it never rates the doctor, and anything dangerous goes straight to a doctor the same day. The phone is a design concept; the medication ledger underneath is already built.

> Notes: Today an Indian insurer prices a policy once and learns nothing until a claim arrives. Nobody owns the layer in between, where risk can actually be reduced. The daily companion produces exactly that data: adherence, activity, check-ins, retests and follow-ups, all consented. We earn the right in three steps: a wellness partner in year one, a risk signal tied to the insurer's own claims in year two, and an underwriting layer in year three through the IRDAI sandbox.
