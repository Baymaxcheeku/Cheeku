# The State of Longevity 2026 (Nozomi): what it means for Cheeku

Source: Nozomi, "The State of Longevity: Business Models, Economics, Retention,
and Scaling of Longevity Companies", 2026, 318 pages. Nozomi is a digital
health product consultancy that sells membership and engagement software, so
cite it for benchmarks, and a16z for the thesis. Read 12 Nov 2026.

## The insights that matter for us

1. **The loop is the product.** Diagnostics are easy to buy; the hard part is
   the operating system around them: activate, orchestrate tests, interpret,
   reduce anxiety, turn findings into actions, close abnormal-result loops,
   show progress. Winning loop: measure, normalise, interpret, prioritise,
   intervene, monitor, remeasure, demonstrate progress.
2. **Diagnostics get cheaper; interpretation captures the margin** (their
   prediction 2, high confidence). Raw measurement stops being the moat.
3. **Architecture:** buy the system of record and integrations; build and own
   the longitudinal data platform, the intelligence layer and the member
   experience. The defining technical problem is data reconciliation (identity,
   units, local lab codes, provenance), not data acquisition.
4. **The renewal cliff.** Year one has the baseline; year two must justify
   itself. Fixes: a mid-year value event (Function tests twice a year) and
   booking the next test before the person leaves (Neko: 75% prepay the next
   scan on the spot, 80% book the third).
5. **Retention follows frequency.** Oura: about 85% 12-month paid retention
   with DAU/MAU about 65%. MDVIP: about 90% a year.
6. **Why members leave:** value decay, invisible benefit, participation burden,
   "nobody knows me", friction. In a 400-person study 36.9% left over
   competing priorities. Engagement fell over time in 77% of digital studies.
7. **What drives adherence:** a personalised plan, visible progress, feedback
   tied to behaviour, human support. Generic push notifications and big
   dashboards without priorities are weak or harmful.
8. **Information hierarchy:** urgent, actionable, monitor, informational,
   experimental. "Patients should never have to determine from a dashboard
   whether a result is an emergency." 58% see results before a clinician;
   only about two thirds of them understand them.
9. **What investors want:** a platform that expands from testing to
   interpretation, coaching, prescribing, monitoring and employer or insurer
   integration; disease-labelled paths (diabetes, obesity, menopause, sleep
   apnoea, frailty) rather than "ageing"; recurring revenue; a distribution
   advantage; clinical seriousness; longitudinal multimodal data.
10. **"Longevity clinics" dissolve into preventive medicine** (prediction 1):
    sell measurable reduction of specific risks, not abstract longevity.
11. **Underrated:** muscle and physical function, the GLP-1 aftermarket,
    women's midlife health (35 to 65), sleep detection to treatment, employer
    channels. **Overhyped:** a single biological-age score, IV NAD, unapproved
    peptides, scan-everyone MRI.
12. **Longevity becomes an employer, payer and government question**
    (prediction 10, 2028 to 2035).
13. **Price tiers:** lab and data memberships sit under $500 to $1,000 a year
    (a $365 lab subscription is cited); physician-led programmes $1,500 to
    $5,000; concierge $7,500 to $25,000+.
14. **Asia-Pacific is "the most important long-term volume market"**, yet the
    report names only Japan, Korea, Singapore and China. India is absent.
15. **Trust as moat:** publish evidence and outcomes; make privacy a product
    feature ("never advertising exhaust"). Durable-business test: would the
    model still make sense if every financial incentive were disclosed?
16. **KPIs that matter:** prepaid annual conversion, 12/24/36-month retention,
    repeat-test attachment, family expansion, contribution margin after
    clinical labour.

## Where Cheeku stands

Ahead or aligned: the closed loop (memory, rules, model, booking, retest);
own the data and intelligence layers, buy fulfilment (LabStack); four urgency
tiers and hard stops match the information hierarchy; family accounts give
"family expansion"; employer and insurer channels match prediction 10; Indian
prices are about a tenth of the cheapest US tier.

Gaps to close:
- Renewal cliff: book the next retest when a report lands; a visible
  six-month progress moment.
- Functional measures (grip, gait, strength, muscle) are missing from the
  longevity engine.
- No explicit women's midlife (35 to 65) path; segments cover PCOS and
  sexual health but not menopause.
- Ads in non-urgent chats sit awkwardly with "privacy as product": state that
  ads never use health data, or drop them from the story.
- Track repeat-test attachment, family expansion and 12-month retention.
- Do not show or market a single biological-age number.
