### What are you building, and why?

> We're building the health memory and care layer for Indian families. Cheeku keeps every report, prescription and reading per family member, answers daily questions against that history, checks each prescription against 10 published standards, and books care in the same chat: home lab tests, doctors, dietitians, physio, across 12,000+ pincodes. Clinician-signed rules decide; the model only explains; it never diagnoses.
>
> Why: Indians live to about 71 but spend more than 10 of those years unwell, and that is decided in the 90 days between two-minute doctor visits. Families carry it alone on WhatsApp and pay 39% of health costs out of pocket. India has 797 million digital health IDs, but the records sit in silos and nobody acts on them over time.
>
> Every answer and booking writes back to the family's memory. That compounding, consented longitudinal data is what lets us train India's own health model (15 to 22B parameters, from 2029) and become the risk layer Indian health insurers have never had.

### What unique insight do you have into this problem?

> Indian healthcare is not managed by patients. It is managed by one person in each family, usually an adult child looking after parents and kids. Every product in India serves the patient in a single episode: a consult, a pharmacy order, a lab test. None serves the family health manager across time, so nobody sees that Papa's HbA1c went 6.9, 7.4, 8.1 across three labs and three doctors.
>
> Two of us worked inside Practo, India's largest health platform, and we know why incumbents won't fix this: they earn when you book a consult or buy medicine, so a companion that sometimes says "rest at home" or flags a prescription works against their revenue. Doctors are Practo's paying customers; Apollo routes everything to Apollo.
>
> Our second insight is about the data. Hospitals see episodes. Pharmacies see purchases. A daily companion sees the 90 days in between, where risk actually changes. That is the dataset that trains a better India health model and prices insurance risk, and it only accrues to whoever earns the family's trust first.

### Who are your competitors? How do you differentiate?

> Families today stitch together four kinds of tools, and none holds the story:
>
> 1. **General AI (ChatGPT Health, Claude):** 230M people a week ask ChatGPT health questions, which proves demand. But a chatbot doesn't keep a structured family history, doesn't know Indian lab formats or medicine brands, and can't book a lab test in Indore.
> 2. **Booking and pharmacy platforms (Practo, Apollo 24/7, Tata 1mg, PharmEasy):** they serve one episode and earn per consult or order, so a companion that says "no visit needed" or questions a prescription cuts against their revenue.
> 3. **Health records apps (Eka Care, ABHA apps):** they store files but don't read them over time or act on them.
> 4. **Wellness apps (HealthifyMe):** one slice, diet and fitness, one person.
>
> Cheeku is the only one that does all four at once: a per-person family memory, a prescription check, care booked in chat, and Indian context underneath. The closest analogue is Ant Group's AI health app in China, which reached national scale. Our edge compounds: every report and booking makes the family's file deeper, and leaving means starting from zero.

### How big is the market? (bottoms-up)

> **Users:** India has 797M digital health IDs. We assume 25% belong to people who manage health online: about 200M people.
>
> **Revenue per user:** our base case earns $4.90 (₹429) per average user a year across care bookings, subscriptions ($1.15 and $2.90 a month for Solo and Family), employer plans, insurance and ads.
>
> **Serviceable market:** 200M × $4.90 = about **$1B a year** in direct revenue.
>
> **The spend we route:** Indian families pay about $41B a year out of pocket for health (39.4% of $104B total health spending, National Health Accounts 2021-22). Cheeku books part of that spend in chat.
>
> **On top:** an insurer risk layer worth about $115M a year at 1% share of health insurance, and model licensing to hospitals.
>
> **Our plan:** 20M average users in 2031 at $4.90 = $99M (₹862 crore) revenue, 72% gross margin, EBITDA positive from May 2030.
