# The ask and the valuation: what to say in the room

Two scripts for the same question ("What's your ask, and how much are you open to sell?"), one for angels and one for institutions. Both are written to be said out loud, in plain words, with numbers anyone can follow. The numbers come from the seed split calculator (https://claude.ai/artifact/Ciyr53bMWYQwH1Hj1CMVSw), the spend model and the financial model, so they match everything else we have sent out.

Today is 5 Nov 2026. Update anything that has moved before you walk in.

## Pitch 1: the angels

> Thanks for making the time. Let me start with what I'm asking, then tell you why.
>
> I'm raising ₹5 crore from a small group of angels. We want it closed in December, just before we open the app to everyone in January. Most people write ₹10 to ₹50 lakh. It goes in as an iSAFE, which just means we don't argue about a valuation today. Your money turns into shares when our bigger round closes in the first quarter. Your price is capped at a ₹85 crore valuation, and if that bigger round comes in lower than we hope, you get 10% off it.
>
> How much of the company am I selling? None of mine. Every share is new. Across the whole ₹26 crore, angels and institutions together take about a fifth. Your ₹5 crore buys about 5%. The institutions' ₹21 crore buys about 16%. The three of us keep roughly 71%.
>
> Here's what that looks like for one cheque. Say you put in ₹25 lakh. If the institutions come in where I'm aiming, which is ₹130 crore after their money, your ₹25 lakh is worth about ₹32 lakh the day it converts. If we hit the numbers in our Series A plan, about ₹58 lakh. That's paper value, and it's a plan, not a promise.
>
> The ₹5 crore has one job. It runs us from December to the end of March. About ₹3.5 crore gets spent and ₹1.5 crore stays in the bank as a cushion. Roughly ₹1.1 crore goes on the team, ₹1.4 crore on getting the first users, ₹60 lakh on one-time things like a licensed drug database, legal and a security audit, and the rest on running costs. By March we expect about 1.3 lakh people using it every month.
>
> Why I think it's worth backing. Indians live about 71 years and stay healthy for about 60. Those last ten years get built quietly. Take someone's fasting sugar over three years: 104, then 114, then 124. Each time a doctor spends two minutes on it and says keep an eye on it. Nobody lines the three up. Cheeku does. It keeps every report and prescription for the whole family, checks every medicine against the others, flags the climb, and books the repeat test at home or a doctor, right in the chat. Our care partners already reach 12,000+ pincodes, so that last step works today.
>
> We built all of it with no outside money. Suraj built clinical AI on real blood samples at SigTuple and a blast-wave system for DRDO. Deepesh ran over ₹1,000 crore of revenue at Pine Labs and grew Practo's enterprise business 2.2 times. I'm a second-time founder, and I worked on Meta's AI as a vendor in London.
>
> And look at who is already writing cheques in this space. Accel and Claypond put $3 million, about ₹27 crore, into August AI last October. They say they're at nine million users now, and they were reported to be in talks with Peak XV this summer. August is a strong product. But its paid care launched in the US in September, and from what I can see, in India it's mostly a free chat that explains things. Nobody in India does the chat, the booking and the memory for a whole family together. That's the gap. You'd be in before the app is public.

## Pitch 2: the institutions

> We're raising ₹26 crore, about $2.7 million. ₹5 crore of that is angels, already in motion. The other ₹21 crore is what I'd like to talk to you about, and I'm looking for one lead who writes ₹10 crore or more.
>
> We're pricing the seed at about ₹130 crore after the money goes in. So ₹21 crore buys about 16%, and with the angels the whole seed is about 21%. It's all new shares. None of the founders are selling, and we keep about 71%. I'm comfortable in that range. I'd rather take a smaller cheque than a lower price.
>
> The ₹26 crore gives us 17 months, to February 2028. About ₹9.4 crore goes on people, ₹9.1 crore on growth, ₹4.7 crore on our own model, and ₹2.9 crore on safety, compliance and drug data. By the Series A we're aiming for 7 lakh monthly users, 12,000 paying subscribers, care booked in the chat every week, and our tuned model beating the big frontier APIs on Indian tests.
>
> I know ₹130 crore before launch needs defending, so here is how I think about it.
>
> One, the category has a price. Accel and Claypond put $3 million, about ₹27 crore, into August AI last October. By the company's own count they're at nine million users, and they were reported to be talking to Peak XV about $4 million more. We're asking for the same size cheque. I'm not saying we're ahead of August. They had 35 lakh users when they raised, and we have none yet. You are backing the launch.
>
> Two, the product is already built, and it cost us nothing. The chat, the family memory, a fine-tuned health model with a safety router, and the longevity engine. Most teams need a year and a real budget to get here.
>
> Three, the hard part to copy is fulfilment. Our care partners reach 12,000+ pincodes, and 5,000+ consultants, labs and medicine services deliver to the home. August's paid care launched in September in the US. From what I can see, in India it's a free chat that explains. We close the loop in the chat: read the report, remember the family, check the medicine, book the test or the doctor.
>
> Four, the team covers the three things this needs. Suraj on clinical-grade AI, Deepesh on healthcare distribution and partnerships, and me on product. We built the whole thing ourselves.
>
> Five, the next step is easy to see. If we hit those numbers, our model puts the Series A at about ₹250 crore before the money. That's nearly twice where you'd come in.
>
> And the returns. In the base case revenue is ₹1.9 crore in 2027 and ₹862 crore in 2031, at a 72% gross margin. Your ₹21 crore shrinks to about 10% after the Series A and B. At three times revenue that's roughly 12 times your money. If we only deliver half, and the market pays two times revenue, it's still about 4 times. I'd rather show you the version where we miss than only the one where we don't.
>
> What I'd like from you is a lead for roughly half the round. I can send the model and the data room tonight.

## Tough questions

**"₹130 crore with no users?"** Say it straight. "You're right, we have no public users yet. You're paying for a built product, a care network that already reaches 12,000+ pincodes, and a team that did this with zero outside money. The same size cheque went into August last year when the category was unproven. The launch in January is what turns this into numbers, and I'd rather you see them before the Series A than after."

**"August has nine million users. Why won't they do this in India?"** "They might, and they have the money. But they've just launched paid care in the US and describe their mission as American healthcare. Delivering in India means local labs, local pincodes, Hindi, and Indian prices, and that is slow work we've already done. We also hold the family memory, which is the part that gets better every month."

**"What if ChatGPT or Google do the chat part?"** "They'll do the answer. They won't do the booking, the clinician-signed rules that decide ranges and risk, or a memory of your whole family built from your reports. Answering is cheap. Acting is the hard part."

**"Why would anyone pay?"** "A family pays ₹249 a month for up to four people, about ₹8 a day for the whole family, or roughly ₹2 a day per person. For that they get every report read and remembered, every prescription checked, and a doctor or a blood test booked in one tap. There's a ₹29 day pass for a single worry. Most of our revenue comes from care booked in the chat, where we add a small markup on the partner's price."

**"What if the institutional round slips?" (angels)** "The ₹5 crore runs the full plan to about April. If I switch to lean mode after March it stretches to about May. The iSAFE converts at your cap at the next priced round, or automatically after three years, and it carries a liquidation preference up to the money you put in."

**"Why this cap?" (angels)** "₹85 crore is about a third below where we expect the institutional seed to price, and it's the lowest cap that keeps the whole seed under our limit if that round comes in weak. I'd rather give early people a fair deal that I can keep than promise a price I might have to reopen."

**"We'd want more than 16%."** "I'd rather take a smaller cheque than a lower price. At ₹90 crore before money, a ₹20 crore cheque still keeps us inside what we can live with. Let's find the cheque size that works."

## For your eyes only

- Our limit is 23% sold across angels and institutions. Do not say the number. Say "about a fifth".
- With the ₹85 cr cap, the seed stays under 23% as long as the institution prices at ₹116 cr post-money or higher (₹95 cr pre-money with the iSAFE inside). Below ₹113 cr post-money no cap fixes it, so shrink the cheque. At ₹90 cr pre-money the largest cheque is about ₹20 cr, at ₹85 cr about ₹18 cr.
- Ask the institution to quote a post-money that includes the iSAFE, and to say in the term sheet that the iSAFE converts inside their pre-money. A pool top-up is a separate negotiation and comes out of the founders.
- Angel sweetener if you need to close faster: the first ₹2 cr at an ₹80 cr cap and the remaining ₹3 cr at ₹90 cr. It blends to almost the same ownership.
- Do not promise any valuation to angels. Say "we are aiming for" and "in our plan".
- If you have a real usage number, put it in. A beta group, a waitlist, or the family's usage over the year. I did not invent one and the scripts rely on being honest that we have no public users yet.
