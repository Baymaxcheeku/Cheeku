Strategic postmortem · 1 Nov 2026 · founders only

Jivi died of a cost structure. Cheeku has a cheaper version of the same one.

A teardown of Jivi Health (JiviAI), the Gurugram AI doctor that shut down in June 2026, and what it means for every product, technology and capital decision at Cheeku. Cheeku numbers come from our own financial model, run again for this page.

Every claim carries its evidence level: Verified primary or named-source reporting Reported secondary or anonymous sources Analysis our inference Model Cheeku financial model v2

The verdict in five lines

  1. Jivi's costs grew with usage before revenue did: proprietary medical models plus a free AI doctor for 3.5M installs in 170+ countries, on about $3M. When the next round failed and the acquisition talks died, nothing was left.
  2. Cheeku is not immune. Our current plan needs ₹246 cr across three rounds, sells consumer subscriptions at LTV/CAC of about 1.0 (0.36 in the bear case), and still budgets an own LLM, a pendant and state contracts. That is Jivi's trap at a different price.
  3. The memory is the right product and the wrong thing to charge for. Families pay for tests and care, not for being remembered. Price the outcome; let the memory do the retention.
  4. Recommended: Hybrid-lean. Orchestrate partner supply, never own it; sell a care plan with tests inside; lead with employers, the one buyer with budget (seat payback about 2.7 months in our model); put the own model behind a revenue gate.
  5. On our model, Hybrid-lean needs ₹96 cr instead of ₹246 cr, turns EBITDA positive in Feb 2030 instead of May 2030, and still reaches break-even in the bear case. The full plan does not.

A · Executive teardown

What happened to Jivi

Jivi was founded by Ankur Jain (ex-BharatPe CPO, WalmartLabs) with G V Sanjay Reddy (GVK Group, GVK EMRI) as chairman, and Andrew Ng's AI Fund as co-founder and investor. It built MedX, a medical LLM that topped a Hugging Face medical leaderboard, and shipped an AI doctor app on Android, iOS and WhatsApp.

Timeline, Oct 2023 to Jun 2026

Dates from the sources listed at the end. Gap that matters: 15 months from public launch to "subscriptions coming soon", and no disclosed paid revenue line before the money ran out.

Verified causes

  • Infrastructure cost of proprietary models. "Building and running proprietary AI models became increasingly expensive. When you're up against companies like OpenAI and Google, it becomes very difficult to make the economics work." Reported (Entrackr, anonymous source)
  • The planned round did not close. Investors who had shown interest did not participate. Reported
  • Acquisition talks failed. Reported
  • The core was free by design. Chairman Reddy, Oct 2024: keep the core free, add monetising features later. Verified
  • Monetisation started late. Jul 2025, Jain: subscriptions "soon"; a marketplace for medicines, supplements and devices "we have just started testing". Verified
  • Breadth. AI doctor, doctor co-pilot, ICD-11 coding, HealthOS, MedX agents, AudioX voice model, open-source releases, 100+ languages, 170+ countries, with 22 to 29 people. Verified

No founder postmortem has been published. Revenue is only known as a Tracxn band (under ₹10 cr, FY25), which tells us nothing.

Our hypotheses, in order of weight

  • H1 · Global free users were cost with no market. You cannot book a lab test in 170 countries. Every non-Indian user was inference spend with no path to revenue. Analysis
  • H2 · Installs were not habit. 3.5M installs, but no retention, conversion or cost-per-user was ever disclosed. Symptom checking is episodic. Analysis
  • H3 · No paying buyer was chosen. Consumers, doctors, hospitals and coders all appeared in the pitch; none got a sales motion. Analysis
  • H4 · Benchmarks are not a product. Beating GPT-4 on USMLE mattered in 2024. By 2026 ChatGPT Health was free and good enough for most questions. Analysis
  • H5 · "AI doctor" capped trust. It invites liability questions, doctor resistance and regulatory attention, and makes paying feel like paying for a fake doctor. Analysis
  • H6 · $3M was app money spent like model money. Andrew Ng himself told ET in Oct 2024 that most of India's AI value would be in applications on foundation models, not in training them. Verified quote

Fundamentally flawed

  • Training and serving proprietary models on a $3M seed
  • Global free distribution with India-only monetisation ideas
  • Positioning as an AI doctor that diagnoses
  • No single buyer with budget, pain and accountability

Premature

  • Doctor co-pilot and ICD-11 coding: a real B2B product that needs a sales team
  • Medicine and device marketplace before habit
  • Own voice model (AudioX) before voice usage existed
  • Wearables and predictive monitoring

Right but commercially hard

  • Report explanation: a strong, repeatable wedge
  • WhatsApp as the front door
  • Indic languages and voice
  • Drug interaction checks and a doctor-validation layer
  • A longevity coach next to the AI doctor

What Jivi needed to change to survive

  • India only, and stop serving countries it could never monetise.
  • API plus fine-tuning instead of training; spend the compute money on distribution.
  • One payer from month one: GVK EMRI, an insurer, or bookings revenue. The chairman ran India's largest ambulance network.
  • Charge for outcomes: a report review by a doctor, a test booked, a consult.
  • Cut to ten people or raise $8 to 10M once the cost curve was clear.
  • Publish retention, not installs, to the next investors.

Reconstructed Jivi burn Analysis · low confidence

Assumptions: 25 people at ₹25 to 35 lakh loaded a year; free inference for about 10% of installs active monthly at ₹0.30 to ₹1 per long, multilingual answer; hosting and training of own models; office and tools. Result: about ₹1.2 to 2.0 cr a month. $3M (about ₹25 cr) lasts 13 to 21 months from Oct 2024, which runs out between Nov 2025 and Jul 2026. The shutdown came in Jun 2026, so the reported story is consistent with the arithmetic.

E · Recommended strategy

Hybrid-lean: charge for care, retain with memory, sell through employers

Are we building the wrong thing? No, but we are planning to charge for the wrong thing and fund too many things at once. The memory is a retention engine and a data asset. It is not a product people pay ₹249 a month for on its own.

Six decisions

  1. Sell a care plan, not chat. Test a Family Care Plan at ₹2,999 to ₹4,999 a year that includes home health checks, a doctor review of every report and unlimited Cheeku. Keep ₹99 and ₹249 as the entry tiers. This revisits the test bundles the thesis dropped in v2, so let the price test decide.
  2. Employers from month one. Do not wait for 1 lakh users. Seats pay back in under three months in our model, and HR teams have the budget, pain and accountability Jivi never found.
  3. Diagnostics first among bookings. Highest frequency, clearest price, and the retest loop is the longevity engine.
  4. Gate the own model. Own GPUs only when chat cost passes 15% of revenue. Own model only after the Series A, ₹20 cr+ revenue run rate and an eval win. The ₹33 cr becomes a decision, not a budget line.
  5. India, two languages, one metro cluster at launch. Hindi and English, Delhi NCR first, then Bengaluru and Mumbai.
  6. Spend like Lightweight until retention proves out. Hold burn near ₹55 lakh a month until day-30 retention passes 25%, then step up.

The smallest version that makes money

Report reader, family memory and home lab booking with retest reminders, sold as the Family Care Plan in Delhi NCR, through 10 to 20 employers. Team of 12, about ₹50 lakh a month. At roughly ₹160 a month contribution per plan (₹2,999 a year less about ₹900 of tests, serving and payments), break-even is about 31,000 paying families. Analysis · low confidence

From Jivi: copy and improve

  • Copy the report explainer as the first-session hook, and WhatsApp as a front door.
  • Copy the doctor-validation story, but make it real: a named clinical lead and weekly chat review.
  • Improve drug checks into our 10-standard prescription scorecard, tied to the family's own medicines.
  • Improve the longevity coach into the retest loop, which earns a booking every quarter.

From Jivi: avoid and rethink

  • Avoid training models, open-source releases and voice models to signal technical depth.
  • Avoid install counts and country counts as the headline.
  • Rethink our own plan's ten engines and three rounds: they repeat Jivi's breadth with more money.
  • Rethink what we charge for: the memory stays free to grow; care earns.

G · How Cheeku could fail just like Jivi

Twelve ways this goes wrong, and the tripwire for each

Free usage outruns revenue

Chat volume grows faster than bookings and plans; serving cost becomes the burn.

Tripwire: serving cost above ₹6 per free user a month. Prevent: route easy questions to small models, cache, cap long-context replies.

Model vanity

We start training our own model because it raises well, before anyone pays.

Tripwire: compute above 10% of burn before the Series A. Prevent: the revenue gate in decision 4.

Benchmark theatre

Investor updates lead with eval scores instead of retention and bookings.

Tripwire: any update without day-30 retention on page one. Prevent: a fixed metric order in every update.

Installs are not habit

Health is episodic; families open Cheeku when sick and forget it otherwise.

Tripwire: day-30 retention under 15%, or under 6 chats per monthly user. Prevent: retest loop, family nudges, WhatsApp delivery.

Nobody pays for memory

The family loves the memory and still will not pay ₹249 for it.

Tripwire: paid conversion under 1% after three months live. Prevent: price care plans and bookings, not access.

Ten engines, no engine

We split 24 people across fitness, ads, insurance, states and hardware.

Tripwire: more than three revenue lines with staff before ₹1 cr annual revenue. Prevent: the do-not-build list.

The Series A slips

Like Jivi's failed round: the plan assumes ₹70 cr at month 13.

Tripwire: under nine months of runway without a term sheet. Prevent: Hybrid-lean spend, and the model's defend mode cuts marketing and compute automatically.

Straddling consumer and enterprise

Insurer, state and hospital deals pull founders off the consumer product too early.

Tripwire: a founder spending over 20% of time on institutional deals before 2028. Prevent: employers only; insurers as a channel pilot.

A trust incident

A missed "go now" or a wrong medicine flag goes viral.

Tripwire: any missed urgent case in the weekly chat review. Prevent: zero-miss release gate, weekly doctor review, liability cover.

A regulator calls it a medical device

CDSCO treats the prescription check or risk scores as software that guides treatment, or a DPDP breach happens.

Tripwire: no written regulatory opinion by launch. Prevent: opinion in Q1; consent ledger; India hosting.

Partners squeeze the take

A lab or doctor partner raises prices or pushes its own app to our users.

Tripwire: any category with one partner, or take rate under 8%. Prevent: two partners per category; customer relationship stays in Cheeku.

ChatGPT Health comes to India with bookings

A free frontier product connects ABHA records and partners with a pharmacy chain.

Tripwire: an OpenAI or Google India health partnership announced. Prevent: win employers and the family graph first; their product serves individuals, ours serves families.

Acquisition as Plan B

Jivi's last exit was a sale that never closed.

Tripwire: treating "someone will buy us" as a runway plan. Prevent: a path to break-even that needs no buyer.

Founder bandwidth

An accelerator in San Francisco, an India launch and a seed raise in the same quarter.

Tripwire: the India launch date moves twice. Prevent: one founder owns launch, one owns the raise, one owns product.
